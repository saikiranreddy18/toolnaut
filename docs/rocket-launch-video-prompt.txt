TOOLNAUT — SIGN-UP LAUNCH SEQUENCE
Text-to-video prompt pack

Use: replaces the coded launch animation in ArrivalLaunch.jsx with a rendered
video. Plays full-screen for ~2.6s after sign-up, then fades to /goal.


================================================================================
1. MAIN PROMPT  (Veo / Sora / Kling / Runway Gen-3 / Hailuo)
================================================================================

16-bit pixel art animation, retro arcade game cutscene. A chunky pixel-art
rocket launches straight upward through deep space, seen from the side, dead
centre of frame.

The rocket: a tall charcoal-grey metal hull with a crisp bright lime-green
outline tracing its entire silhouette. Pointed dark-grey nose cone at the top
with a lime seam where it meets the body. Two short swept-back fins low on the
sides. A small flat nozzle at the base.

Set into the middle of the hull is a large circular porthole with a thick
light-blue-grey metal ring and dark navy glass. Behind the glass, a friendly
little green alien with two antennae, large round white eyes with black pupils,
and a small smile, looking out at the viewer. The alien bobs very slightly with
the rocket's vibration.

From the nozzle, a bright rainbow exhaust plume streams downward: a lime-green
core, an orange middle band, and blue outer edges, all in hard-edged pixel
blocks that flicker and pulse rapidly. Small pixel sparks scatter off the sides
of the flame.

Background: near-black deep space with a faint dark-grey diagonal diamond grid.
Small lime-green pixel stars and tiny white pixel dots stream downward past the
rocket at speed, creating the sense of fast upward flight. A couple of small
green pixel planets drift by.

Camera: locked off, no camera movement. The rocket stays centred and rises
slowly while the star field rushes downward. Crisp hard pixel edges, no motion
blur, no anti-aliasing, limited retro colour palette, high contrast.

text, words, letters, numbers, logo, watermark, signature, UI, subtitles,

================================================================================
3. SETTINGS
================================================================================

Aspect ratio ....... 16:9 for desktop. Render a 9:16 pass too — the launch is
                     full-screen and phones are the majority of sign-ups.
Duration ........... 4s (the app uses ~2.6s; the extra gives trim room)
FPS ................ 24 is plenty. Pixel art reads worse at 60 — it smooths.
Resolution ......... 1080p. Do NOT upscale with an AI enhancer afterwards;
                     every one of them softens pixel edges, which is the whole
                     look. If you must resize, use nearest-neighbour.
Loop ............... not needed — it plays once and fades out.
Seed ............... keep the seed of the take you like, so the 9:16 version
                     can match the 16:9 one.

NO TEXT IN THE VIDEO. The "T∞lnaut" wordmark and the IGNITION / LIFTOFF /
WELCOME ABOARD beats stay as DOM text over the top. Video models render text
badly, and keeping it in the DOM means it stays crisp, themeable, and readable
to a screen reader.


================================================================================
4. SHORT VARIANT  (for models with tight prompt limits — Pika, Luma)
================================================================================

16-bit pixel art, retro arcade. A charcoal-grey rocket with a bright lime-green
outline launches straight up, centred. Large round porthole with a blue-grey
metal ring; a cute green alien with antennae, big white eyes and a smile looks
out through it. Rainbow pixel exhaust below — lime core, orange middle, blue
edges — flickering fast. Near-black space, faint diagonal grid, lime pixel
stars streaming downward at speed. Locked camera, hard pixel edges, no blur.


================================================================================
5. FIRST-FRAME REFERENCE
================================================================================

Most models take an image as a start frame, which will hold the design far
tighter than words alone. The coded rocket already renders exactly this — grab
a still from the running app rather than describing it again:

  1. Sign up with a fresh browser profile (or clear exus_quiz_v1)
  2. Screenshot the launch screen during the first second
  3. Feed it as the start/reference frame with the prompt above

That keeps Naut on-model, since he is the existing mascot and not something the
video model should be inventing.


================================================================================
6. WIRING IT IN
================================================================================

ArrivalLaunch.jsx already owns the full-screen overlay, the timing, the beats
and the reduced-motion skip. Only the visual layer changes:

  - drop the file in as public/launch.webm (plus an .mp4 fallback — Safari)
  - swap <PixelRocket /> and the plume/grid/stars for one <video>:
        autoPlay muted playsInline preload="auto"
  - keep the wordmark and beat text where they are, over the video
  - KEEP the reduced-motion branch. It skips the sequence entirely, and a
    full-screen video of a rocket climbing is exactly what that setting is for.
  - KEEP a still-image fallback on the <video> poster, so a failed or blocked
    video load shows a frame rather than a black screen for 2.6 seconds.

Budget note: 4s of video at 1080p is roughly 300-600KB as webm. That is
comparable to the current three.js chunk, but it is a blocking first-paint asset
on the sign-up path, so preload it on the sign-in screen rather than on arrival.
